How to read this

A monopolist faces the whole demand curve, which is its average revenue . Selling one more unit lowers the price on all units, so marginal revenue lies below . The firm produces where , charges the price on the demand curve above that output, and earns the grey rectangle . A price taker facing the same demand would produce more at a lower price (open circle).

A firm with a product of its own is a local monopolist and earns a profit. Rivals then bring out substitutes: they cannot copy the product, but they take some of its demand, so the firm's average revenue curve shifts down. Entry goes on while there is profit. In the long run just touches : zero profit, at an output where and average cost is still falling.
